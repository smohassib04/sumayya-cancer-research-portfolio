Project 01

Biomarker Testing Access

HB 885 · Florida · 2024

01

Problem

Access to biomarker testing can affect whether patients receive information that helps guide cancer treatment.

Research

Investigated the role of biomarker testing and identified gaps in coverage and access affecting Florida patients.

R + Data Analysis

Used R, statistical analysis, and research review to organize cancer and health policy information, examine coverage gaps, and translate findings into evidence that could support a legislative response.

Policy Solution

Florida enacted HB 885 in 2024.

Implementation

The law established coverage requirements for clinically supported biomarker testing under Florida Medicaid and state employee health plans.

Effect

HB 885 changed coverage requirements for clinically supported biomarker testing under Florida Medicaid and state employee health plans. The policy effect is a change in what eligible plans must cover; its effect on testing use and patient outcomes requires evaluation.

Meaning

When biomarker testing is unavailable or unaffordable, patients may be more likely to proceed without clinically relevant information that could help inform treatment decisions, limiting the evidence available to guide care.

“How can evidence about biomarker testing be translated into policy that improves access to clinically supported testing?”

Project 02

Breast Diagnostic Imaging Costs

SB 158 · Florida · 2025

02

Problem

Follow-up breast diagnostic imaging can create out-of-pocket costs for patients after an abnormal screening result.

Research

Reviewed evidence concerning breast diagnostic imaging costs and barriers to follow-up care.

R + Data Analysis

Used R, quantitative analysis, and research review to examine the relationship between out-of-pocket costs, follow-up barriers, and access to diagnostic breast care.

Policy Solution

Florida enacted SB 158 in 2025.

Implementation

The law addresses cost-sharing for diagnostic and supplemental breast examinations within Florida’s state group insurance program and took effect January 1, 2026.

Effect

SB 158 addresses cost-sharing for diagnostic and supplemental breast examinations within Florida’s state group insurance program. Its effect on follow-up care and detection outcomes should be measured rather than assumed.

Meaning

When cost or coverage barriers make follow-up harder after an abnormal screening result, patients may be more likely to delay or forgo recommended diagnostic evaluation. That delay can leave a potentially important finding unresolved longer, which is detrimental to timely cancer detection and care.

“How can research on financial barriers to cancer detection inform policy designed to improve access to follow-up breast care?”
